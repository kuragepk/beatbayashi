# What you may do with what you make, in English

**Version**: 1.2 (2026-10-06)

> This page explains, in English, what you are allowed to do with the music you
> make here, and why. It is not the project's governing document: that is
> [PROJECT-POLICY.md](PROJECT-POLICY.md), in Japanese, and it wins wherever the
> two differ. This page used to carry the same filename as that document while
> saying different things.
>
> This is not legal advice. It gives you facts and the means to check them.

## Part 1: What You Own

### Your Music

Everything you make in BeatBayashi is yours.

- You may sell it, publish it, monetize it on YouTube, license it for film or games, or keep it private.
- You do not need our permission and you do not need to credit us.
- The app is a tool, not a content creator. Your patterns, your sounds (when edited), and your exported audio are your work, not ours.

### Our Patterns

The 72 built-in rhythm patterns are generic groove patterns, not transcriptions of copyrighted songs. They are named after genres (House, Techno, etc.) but are not legal "copies" of any specific work. A four-on-the-floor kick pattern is not owned by anyone.

- The patterns themselves (their step grids, tempos, names and descriptions) are offered under [CC0 1.0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/) or [MIT-0](https://opensource.org/license/mit-0), whichever you choose. The project gives up or licenses away any rights it has in them, so you can use them anywhere without asking or crediting us. The code that plays them is MIT-0. Patterns you write or change yourself are yours.
- When you export audio, MIDI, SFZ or a pattern file, the project claims no rights in it. Exported pattern, MIDI and SFZ files say so inside the file and link the CC0 1.0 deed; a WAV file has no room for that text.

### Our Sounds

Every drum sound in this app is generated by synthesis, not sampled from recordings. This means:

- No third-party recording is embedded in the app or in your export.
- There are no master recording copyrights, neighbouring rights, or session musician credits to clear.
- Your export is safe to release anywhere without a sample clearance.
- The built-in sound presets (their parameter values) are offered under CC0 1.0 or MIT-0, whichever you choose.

## Part 2: Licence of the Software

### Code

All source code written for this project is released under MIT No Attribution (MIT-0) ([LICENSE](../LICENSE)). It replaced the MIT licence on 2026-10-06. The permission sentence reads:

```
Permission is hereby granted, free of charge, to any person obtaining a copy of this
software and associated documentation files (the "Software"), to deal in the Software
without restriction, including without limitation the rights to use, copy, modify,
merge, publish, distribute, sublicense, and/or sell copies of the Software, and to
permit persons to whom the Software is furnished to do so.
```

In plain English: You can use it, modify it, sell it, and include it in other projects. Unlike the ordinary MIT licence, MIT-0 does not ask you to keep a licence notice, even when you redistribute the source code.

A few files keep a different licence. The theme kit files (not their hand-written `.d.ts` type files) are copies of the owner's shared UI templates, which are MIT, and the local `sanitize-filename` stand-in is also MIT. The licence texts quoted in `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt` belong to their authors. `REUSE.toml` lists which is which.

### Dependencies

The web app's runtime dependencies are MIT, except `tslib`, which is 0BSD. Two of them, `@otoinki/loudness` and `@otoinki/offline-render`, are the owner's own shared packages and are also MIT. MIT asks you to keep their licence notices when you redistribute the app; `THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt` collects the notices for the bundled code. The backend's direct dependencies are Apache-2.0. The mobile app's direct dependencies are MIT, but the tree Expo installs for building also contains other licences (for example BSD, ISC, Apache-2.0, MPL-2.0 and CC-BY-4.0); these are build tools and data, and none of them applies to the music you make. See `dependency-licenses.csv` and `PROVENANCE.md`; check the installed tree yourself with `pnpm licenses list --prod` in each app folder.

## Part 3: Your Data

### Web App

- Patterns and settings are stored locally in your browser's localStorage. They never leave your computer.
- No analytics: we do not track which patterns you use, how long you sit in the app, or anything about your session.
- No accounts: there is no login, no profile, no data server. The app works entirely offline after the first load.
- No ads (current version). If advertising is added in a future version, it will be:
  - Opt-in (you enable it; the app defaults to ad-free)
  - Privacy-respecting (no tracking pixels, no third-party data sales)

### Plugin

There is no plugin (see DAW compatibility in Part 7).

### Exports

When you export MIDI, JSON, or audio:
- MIDI: a Standard MIDI File. No metadata, no tracking. It is a sequence of note events and a tempo.
- JSON: your pattern data in plaintext. You can read it, edit it, paste it anywhere.
- Audio (WAV): unencrypted, uncompressed PCM audio. No watermarks, no DRM.

## Part 4: Money

Today: the app is free, there is nothing to buy, and there is no advertising.
That is the whole factual answer, and it is checkable: there is no payment code,
no ad code and no telemetry in this repository.

Everything beyond that (a paid plugin, sample packs, an ad-supported version of the web app)
has been discussed, but not decided and not built. An earlier version of this page
described prices and paid plans as though they existed; they never have. Under the
project's fourth promise (say plainly what is not known), none of it belongs
here until it ships. If money ever enters the picture, it will be written down
here first, with the date.

What will not change: what you make is yours, free of charge, whatever
happens to the app around it.

## Part 5: Privacy & Ethics

### We Do Not

- Sell your data to third parties
- Track your patterns or editing habits
- Require an account or login
- Store anything on our servers
- Use cookies to follow you across sites
- Run analytics on your usage
- Build profiles for advertising purposes
- Require you to agree to terms longer than this document

### We Do

- Assume you own what you make
- Respect offline privacy as the default
- Keep the code open so you can audit it
- Invite contributions and forks
- Document our dependencies transparently
- Update this policy if something changes (with notice)

### If You Distrust Us

The entire app is open-source. You can:
- Fork it and host your own version
- Audit the code yourself
- Build the app yourself
- Understand exactly what data is (and isn't) being sent

## Part 6: Attribution & Contribution

### You Don't Need to Credit Us

Using BeatBayashi in your music does not require mentioning the app. You own the result.

### We Welcome Forks & Contributions

- If you improve the app, you can contribute back via pull request.
- If you fork it, MIT-0 asks nothing of you. We would be glad if you kept it open.
- If you build a plugin using our code, you keep it open-source or release it commercially; it is up to you, and you owe nothing back.

### Citation (If You Want To)

If you want to credit the project (not required), cite it as:

> Drums by BeatBayashi

Or simpler: "Made with BeatBayashi."

## Part 7: Liability & Warranty

### The MIT-0 licence

The software is provided "as-is" without warranty. We make no guarantee that:
- It will not crash
- It will produce sonically pleasant sounds
- It will export correctly
- It will work forever

In plain English: Use at your own risk. Make backups. If the app loses your pattern, that is not our fault, though we will try to help.

### DAW compatibility

There is no plugin. A VST3/AU build has been sketched in
`ARCHITECTURE.md` and nothing has been built, so no DAW
compatibility can be claimed at all.

What does work today is export: MIDI (the General MIDI drum map, so it opens in
any DAW), WAV, and a JSON sound recipe. If an export does not load somewhere it
should, that is a bug worth filing.

## Part 8: Future Changes to This Policy

If we change this policy:

1. We will announce the change publicly (in the app, on the GitHub releases page, etc.)
2. We will give users 30 days notice before the new policy takes effect
3. Users who disagree can continue using an older version of the app under the old policy
4. The policy change will not retroactively affect music you've already made

## Part 9: Questions?

- I want to redistribute the app: fork it. MIT-0 needs no notice.
- I want to use the app commercially: yes. No permission needed. No payment owed.
- I found a bug: file an issue on GitHub or email us.
- I found a privacy leak: email us immediately (see SECURITY.md).
- I want to contribute code: pull requests welcome.
- I want to add sample packs: propose them in a discussion. They must be CC0 (or public domain), so that they can be offered under the same terms as the built-in presets. CC-BY is not accepted, because it would pass an attribution duty on to you (see PROVENANCE.md §5).
- I'm building a DAW and want to embed this: MIT-0 allows it with no conditions. Let us know, and we will help.
